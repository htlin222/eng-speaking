# Day 19 — Goals & Ambitions

## Sentences

- I've arrived at a point where I'm less interested in the conventional markers of success than I once thought I'd be.
- What I'm working toward is harder to explain than a title or a number, which is both its value and its frustration.
- I want to be doing something that demands the fullest version of what I'm capable of. I haven't found it yet, but I know what it feels like when I'm close.
- I've stopped measuring progress by the metrics that are easy to measure, which means I have to rely on something harder to articulate.
- I think about the kind of work I want to be doing in a decade and work backward from there rather than forward from here.
- The ambition was always present. What took time was learning to trust it.
- I've become considerably less patient with the version of myself that waits for conditions to improve before committing.
- I'd rather be wrong about a direction I chose deliberately than right about one I drifted into.
- What I want is to have earned a particular kind of respect for a particular kind of work. That's the closest I can get to a specific answer.
- I've made peace with the fact that this is a longer road than I'd initially anticipated. I think it's the right one.


## Dialog

> A: Where do you see yourself in five years? And I mean genuinely, not the interview answer.
> B: Doing something I built. Doesn't have to be big — just mine.
> A: That's more honest than most people's answers.
> B: Most people give the answer they think they should have. I've stopped doing that.
> A: What changed?
> B: I got tired of performing ambition I didn't actually feel.

## Note

**"Direction rather than destination"** acknowledges that goals evolve. It's a frame for people who have drive but haven't yet found a fixed endpoint — and that's most people.
